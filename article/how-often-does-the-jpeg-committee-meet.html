How Often Does the JPEG Committee Meet?

The Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG) committee convenes four times a year to maintain, develop, and update its internationally recognized imaging standards. Operating under the official designation ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1, the group brings together global imaging experts, academics, and industry representatives to evaluate new compression technologies, resolve interoperability challenges, and ensure that both legacy and emerging image formats meet modern technical demands.

Meeting Schedule and Organization

The committee adheres to a quarterly schedule, holding plenary sessions typically in January, April, July, and October. These week-long meetings cycle between locations in Europe, North America, and Asia to ensure equitable access for international members, though virtual and hybrid formats are also standard practice to maintain active global participation.

Objectives of the Quarterly Meetings

Each meeting is dedicated to progressing image coding standards through formal stages established by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). Core activities during these sessions include:

  • Evaluating Technical Contributions: Reviewing proposals, algorithm designs, and performance benchmarks submitted by member organizations.
  • Drafting Standard Specifications: Advancing documents through formal ISO/IEC stages, from Working Drafts (WD) and Committee Drafts (CD) to Draft International Standards (DIS).
  • Maintaining Legacy Formats: Addressing errata, backward compatibility concerns, and security revisions for established standards like the original 1992 JPEG format.
  • Developing Next-Generation Formats: Innovating solutions for high-performance use cases, including low-latency transmission (JPEG XS), ultra-high-efficiency compression (JPEG XL), and artificial intelligence integration (JPEG AI).

Intersession Activity

While the formal plenary gatherings occur four times per year, technical work continues uninterrupted between meetings. Sub-groups and Ad Hoc Groups (AHGs) collaborate continuously via online forums, teleconferences, and collaborative coding repositories. This continuous workflow ensures that when the full committee convenes, data and draft standards are sufficiently mature for formal voting and approval.
