Why JPEG Still Dominates Embedded Device Compatibility

Despite the advent of modern image formats like WebP, HEIC, and AVIF, the JPEG standard remains the undisputed leader in cross-platform compatibility for embedded smart devices. This article examines the technological and economic factors behind JPEG's staying power, focusing on dedicated silicon decoders, minimal memory requirements, royalty-free licensing, and decades of entrenched firmware standards across microcontrollers, IoT sensors, and edge hardware.

Hardwired Silicon Support

Most microcontrollers (MCUs) and low-power Systems-on-Chip (SoCs)—such as those from STMicroelectronics, NXP, and Espressif—integrate dedicated hardware blocks specifically designed to decode and encode baseline JPEG images. Because this logic is etched directly into the silicon, compressing or decompressing a JPEG requires minimal general-purpose CPU intervention. Newer codecs rely on complex algorithms designed for multi-core processors and modern GPUs, making them computationally impractical for cheap, low-power edge nodes.

Minimal Memory and Processing Footprint

Embedded smart devices often operate within extreme hardware constraints, frequently possessing less than a few megabytes—or even kilobytes—of RAM. The baseline JPEG compression algorithm processes images in discrete \(8 \times 8\) pixel blocks, allowing stream-based decoding with very small memory buffers. Conversely, advanced formats like AVIF and HEIF require large frame buffers to perform complex intra-frame predictions, quickly exhausting the volatile memory available on IoT hardware.

Mature, Ultra-Lightweight Software Libraries

Over thirty years of optimization have produced lightweight, battle-tested open-source libraries such as libjpeg, nanojpeg, and TJpgDec. These libraries can be compiled into firmware binaries measuring mere kilobytes, easily fitting into limited NOR flash memory. Developers building embedded firmware can integrate a stable, bug-free JPEG engine without the maintenance burden and flash-storage overhead associated with modern, fast-evolving codec specifications.

Zero Licensing Friction

The core patents surrounding baseline JPEG expired decades ago, making the format universally accessible without licensing costs or legal risks. In contrast, modern formats frequently carry intellectual property ambiguities or complex patent pools, as seen with HEVC/HEIC. For original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) producing millions of low-margin smart devices, JPEG eliminates both financial overhead and regulatory compliance hurdles.

Universal End-to-End Interoperability

Smart devices rarely operate in isolation; they transmit image payloads to web dashboards, legacy industrial servers, local gateway devices, and mobile apps. Transmitting a JPEG guarantees that any receiving client—regardless of operating system, browser version, or hardware age—can render the image instantly without server-side transcoding. By standardizing on JPEG, embedded systems maintain reliable, real-time data pipelines across disparate ecosystems.
