When Was the Joint Photographic Experts Group Formed?

The Joint Photographic Experts Group, universally known as JPEG, was formally established in 1986 to create an international standard for compressing digital still images. This article covers the origins of the committee, the global organizations responsible for its creation, the technological necessity behind its founding, and how its work culminated in the ubiquitous image format widely used across digital media today.

The group was formed in 1986 through a joint collaboration between the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Telegraph and Telephone Consultative Committee (CCITT, known today as the ITU-T). The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) also joined the effort through its joint technical committee with ISO (ISO/IEC JTC 1). The objective was to bring together telecommunications experts and computing standard bodies to devise an efficient, universal method for transmitting photographic imagery.

Before the committee's formation, digital images required massive storage capacities and bandwidth, making transmission over early computer and telecommunications networks impractical. The JPEG committee was tasked with developing a compression algorithm that could significantly reduce the file size of full-color and grayscale continuous-tone images without a noticeable loss of visual quality.

After several years of research, algorithm submissions, and testing, the group completed its core compression specification. In 1992, the official standard was approved and published as ISO/IEC 10918-1 and ITU-T Recommendation T.81. Today, the committee continues to operate under ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1, maintaining the original JPEG standard and developing modern imaging standards such as JPEG 2000, JPEG XS, and JPEG XL.
