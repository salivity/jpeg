Why Sports Photographers Prefer In-Camera JPEG Over RAW

In the high-stakes world of professional sports photography, capturing the defining moment is only half the battle; the other half is delivering that image to news outlets, wire services, and social media desks within seconds. While RAW format remains the gold standard for dynamic range and post-processing flexibility in portrait or commercial work, sports photographers overwhelmingly rely on in-camera JPEG transmission. This workflow prioritizes instantaneous file transfer, reduced bandwidth load, uninterrupted continuous shooting, and advanced in-camera processing that meets the immediate editorial demands of live event coverage.

The Race Against the Clock

For wire services like the Associated Press, Reuters, and Getty Images, the fastest picture wins. When a game-winning goal is scored, photo editors expect the image to be published online before the celebration on the field even concludes. Transferring a heavy RAW file—typically 40 to 80 megabytes—takes valuable time. In contrast, an optimized JPEG file sits between 5 to 15 megabytes, allowing it to clear transmission queues in fractions of a second.

In-Camera FTP Transmission

Modern flagship cameras used in sports—such as the Nikon Z9, Canon EOS R3, and Sony A1—come equipped with built-in Ethernet ports, robust Wi-Fi, and 5G cellular tethering options. Photographers set up automated background transfers using File Transfer Protocol (FTP).

By assigning a specific button on the camera to "Send to FTP" or by simply rating a photo with a star, the camera pushes that specific frame directly to a remote editor off-site. Delivering a RAW file over an often-congested stadium network creates bottlenecks, whereas JPEGs transmit reliably without interrupting the photographer's focus.

Buffer Management and Burst Rates

Sports action unfolds in milliseconds, requiring continuous burst shooting rates of 12 to 30 frames per second or higher. Shooting uncompressed RAW at these speeds fills a camera's memory buffer rapidly, leading to lockups where the shutter refuses to trip until data writes to the memory card. JPEGs allow photographers to shoot virtually unlimited bursts, ensuring they never miss a critical play while waiting for the buffer to clear.

High-Fidelity Modern Image Processing

Historically, JPEGs were seen as inferior due to limited bit-depth and baked-in white balance. However, modern professional mirrorless sensors and processors generate JPEGs with exceptional dynamic range, accurate skin tones, and controlled noise reduction. When paired with custom camera picture profiles tailored to stadium lighting conditions, an in-camera JPEG requires little to no post-production editing.

Streamlined Remote Editor Workflow

In most major sports venues, photographers do not edit their own work. Instead, an editor in a media room or another country receives the transmitted JPEG directly into an automated software workflow. The editor quickly crops, applies pre-formatted caption metadata, and pushes the photograph directly to media clients worldwide. Because JPEGs require no RAW demosaicing or conversion, this entire round-trip workflow from shutter click to web publication frequently takes under sixty seconds.
