Who Created the Original JPEG Specification?

This article examines the origins of the JPEG image format, detailing the specific joint committee established to develop the standard, the international organizations involved in its creation, and how their collaboration resulted in the universal digital imaging standard recognized today.

The original JPEG specification was created by the Joint Photographic Experts Group, formally designated as ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1.

In the mid-1980s, the need emerged for an efficient, standardized method to compress and transmit continuous-tone still images across telecommunications and computer platforms. To prevent competing proprietary standards, two major international standards bodies joined forces in 1986:

  • The International Organization for Standardization (ISO), together with the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) via their joint technical committee (ISO/IEC JTC 1).
  • The International Telegraph and Telephone Consultative Committee (CCITT), which later became the ITU-T (the Telecommunication Standardization Sector of the International Telecommunication Union).

Together, these organizations established the Joint Photographic Experts Group to develop an international compression standard for grayscale and color still images.

Led by committee members representing research institutes, telecommunications companies, and computer hardware manufacturers, the group selected a discrete cosine transform (DCT) technique proposed by an international consensus.

The committee finalized the baseline JPEG specification in 1992. It was officially published by the ITU-T as Recommendation T.81 in September 1992, and by ISO/IEC as ISO/IEC 10918-1 in 1994. Today, ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1 continues to maintain the standard and develop newer imaging technologies under the JPEG moniker.
