ISO/IEC Working Group for the JPEG Standard

The JPEG format is the universal standard for digital image compression, developed and maintained through international collaboration between global standardization bodies. This article details the official organizational hierarchy responsible for the format, specifically identifying the dedicated working group within ISO/IEC Joint Technical Committee 1 (JTC 1) that oversees the creation, development, and evolution of the JPEG standard.

The working group officially responsible for the JPEG standard within ISO/IEC JTC 1 is ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1, formally titled "Coding of still pictures."

To understand this designation, it helps to examine the hierarchy within the standard-setting framework:

  • ISO/IEC JTC 1: Joint Technical Committee 1 is a collaborative effort between the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) tasked with developing worldwide standards in Information Technology.
  • SC 29: Subcommittee 29 operates under JTC 1 and handles the "Coding of audio, picture, multimedia and hypermedia information." SC 29 oversees several working groups responsible for global media standards, including MPEG and JPEG.
  • WG 1: Working Group 1 operates directly under SC 29 and is specifically charged with the coding and standardization of digital still images.

While ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 1 is its formal bureaucratic title, the committee is colloquially known as the Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG). The term "Joint" reflects the group's ongoing collaboration with the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), specifically ITU-T Study Group 16. Together, experts from both ISO/IEC and ITU-T collaborate to ensure that still picture coding formats are interoperable across computing and telecommunications infrastructures.

In addition to maintaining the original legacy JPEG format (standardized as ISO/IEC 10918 and ITU-T Recommendation T.81), WG 1 continues to research, develop, and standardize newer image coding formats and related technologies. These include modern standards such as JPEG 2000, JPEG XR, JPEG XS, and JPEG XL.
